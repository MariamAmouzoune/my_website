Principal Component Analysis of the AM2017 Barley Panel

Explore genome-wide genetic variation in 316 barley genotypes and understand how PCA supports population-structure assessment for leaf-rust association mapping.

Principal component analysis (PCA) reduces a high-dimensional SNP dataset to a small number of axes that summarize major patterns of genetic variation. In the published AM2017 study, PCA was used to visualize relationships among 316 barley genotypes and to assess population structure before testing marker associations with seedling and adult-plant resistance to Puccinia hordei.

The paper reports that PC1 explained 21.10% of total variation and PC2 explained 9.29%. Together, the first two components represented 30.39% of the variation captured by the PCA.

The article used SNPs retained after filtering out monomorphic markers, markers with minor allele frequency below 5%, and markers with more than 10% missing data. PCA was computed with PLINK 1.9 and the scatter plot was visualized in R using ggplot2.

02

How to interpret the PCA plot

  • Each point represents a genotype; points close together have more similar patterns along the displayed principal components than points far apart.
  • Separated clouds may indicate genetic differentiation, while overlap suggests that the displayed PCs do not clearly separate those genotypes.
  • PC1 and PC2 capture the largest and second-largest axes of variation, not specific genes or causal resistance mechanisms.
  • Coloring points by a metadata group can reveal whether that group aligns with genetic variation, but the visual pattern should not be overinterpreted.
03

Connection to association mapping

Population structure can confound GWAS if allele frequencies and resistance phenotypes differ among genetically differentiated groups. The study compared models that included Q, kinship K, and/or PCA covariates. TASSEL MLM(K+PCA) was selected for the final association analysis because the mixed models controlled false positives more effectively than the tested GLM alternatives.

PCA is therefore both a descriptive tool for understanding genetic relationships and a potential source of covariates for association models; it does not by itself identify leaf-rust resistance loci.

ℹ️
Result versus template

The percentages above are reported by the article. Any newly generated PCA plot should be labeled as a reproduction only after the original marker set, sample order, filtering, and software settings have been matched. Do not claim that PCA separates two-row and six-row types unless the plot and metadata support that specific conclusion.
