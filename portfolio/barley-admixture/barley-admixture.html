Population Structure and Ancestry in the AM2017 Barley Panel

Interpret ancestry components in a diverse barley panel and understand how population structure is used to control confounding in leaf-rust GWAS.

This project interprets the population-structure analysis reported in Amouzoune et al. (2022), which studied the AM2017 panel of 316 barley genotypes (released cultivars, advanced breeding lines and landraces). Population structure is important because ancestry differences can correlate with both marker frequencies and disease response, creating spurious marker–trait associations if not accounted for.

Published analysis: after SNP quality control, 36,793 markers were retained for genetic analyses. A subset of 2,065 informative SNPs, selected using gene diversity and polymorphic information content, was used for ancestry analysis in STRUCTURE 2.3.4 under the admixture model.

The reported STRUCTURE runs used a burn-in of 100,000 and 100,000 MCMC iterations. STRUCTURE HARVESTER was used to examine ΔK and infer a supported number of clusters. Genotypes were assigned to a subgroup when membership was at least 80%; otherwise, they were treated as admixed.

02

Interpretation

What an ancestry bar plot means

  • Each vertical bar represents one barley genotype; colored segments represent estimated membership coefficients for inferred ancestral clusters.
  • A genotype with one dominant component has strong model-based assignment to that cluster. Several substantial components indicate an admixed assignment under the chosen model.
  • The inferred clusters summarize genetic structure in this panel; they are not necessarily formal taxonomic groups or proof of a specific pedigree.
  • The number of clusters should be supported by ΔK, repeated-run consistency, and biological context—not selected solely because a bar plot looks clear.
03

Why this matters for leaf-rust GWAS

Ancestry can influence allele frequencies and may also be associated with differences in disease response. Including a suitable population-structure covariate helps reduce confounding. In the paper, the STRUCTURE membership estimates (Q) informed subgroup assignment and the TASSEL GLM(Q)/MLM(K+Q) model comparisons; the final TASSEL GWAS model was MLM(K+PCA). GAPIT3 model comparisons were also reported.

The Q matrix must be matched to phenotype and genotype records by sample ID, not by assuming that row order is unchanged after filtering.

⚠️
Reproducibility note

This page explains the published STRUCTURE analysis. An sNMF/LEA workflow is a different method and should not be described as the exact method used in the article. Do not report a selected K or individual assignments unless the corresponding published output or rerun results have been checked.
