Linkage Disequilibrium and Resolution of Leaf-Rust Associations in Barley

Understand how linkage disequilibrium (LD) connects nearby SNPs and affects the interpretation and resolution of GWAS signals in the AM2017 barley panel.

Linkage disequilibrium (LD) describes the non-random association of alleles at different loci. In association mapping, a significant SNP can tag a nearby causal variant because the alleles are inherited together more often than expected under independence. LD therefore influences the resolution and transferability of GWAS signals.

This project is framed around interpreting marker–trait associations for leaf-rust resistance in the AM2017 barley panel. It should not be presented as a published genome-wide LD-decay analysis: the article's main reported outcomes are 58 significant marker–trait associations (MTAs), not a single genome-wide LD-decay distance.

01

Context from the published study

58 MTAs across seedling and adult-plant resistance

The study combined disease-response phenotypes with 36,793 SNP markers and identified 58 significant MTAs: 34 associated with seedling resistance to the SRT-MRC and SRT-SAT isolates, and 24 associated with adult-plant resistance assessed at Sidi Allal Tazi in 2017 and 2019. Genomic regions on chromosome 2H (106.53 cM and 107.37 cM) and chromosome 7H (126.7 cM) were reported as shared between seedling and adult-plant resistance stages.

These overlaps indicate that some genomic regions may contribute to resistance measured at different stages. They do not, by themselves, prove that the same causal gene is responsible for every association.

02

How to interpret LD

  • High r²: two markers carry similar allele information in the panel and may tag the same underlying region.
  • Low r²: the markers provide less redundant information; a significant association at one marker should not automatically be extended to another.
  • Physical proximity is not sufficient: nearby markers may have weak LD, and markers farther apart can sometimes show LD due to population history or structure.
  • GWAS signal is not causality: an associated marker identifies a genomic clue, not proof that the SNP itself changes resistance.
